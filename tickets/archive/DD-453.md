# DD-453

## Title

Let Fit all wrap columns onto more than one row.

## Source

Owner suggestion during the cards-spilling conversation (message 3007):
take the columns out of the board section so Fit can wrap them onto
several rows at a readable width.

## Why it needed its own ticket

Once the overflow defects were fixed, thirteen columns across a 1280px
window is 81px each. That is arithmetic, not a defect — every column is
correct, and every column is unusable. Wrapping is the only way to make
a wide board readable without scrolling sideways.

But the board was a `<table>`, and a table cannot wrap: the header row
and the cell row are separate, so a wrapped column would leave its
heading behind. Fixing that means each column owning its own heading,
which is a rebuild of the surface that the card drag, the sticky
headings, the column dropdown, paging, the filter, the add-card control
and the new column editor all read.

## Design

- Each column is now one block holding its own heading and its own
  cards, instead of a header cell in one row and a body cell in
  another.
- Standard mode lays those blocks out in a row that scrolls sideways,
  exactly as before, at the same fixed width.
- Fit mode lays them out as a grid that fills the width and wraps, at a
  readable minimum, so a wide board becomes several rows instead of one
  unreadable one.
- Almost nothing else had to change: the drag finds the list under the
  pointer by geometry, and paging, filtering and counts address columns
  by name. One selector paired headings with lists positionally and now
  reads the heading class instead.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Standard keeps one row, full-width columns and horizontal scroll.
2. Fit wraps onto more than one row, none narrower than readable, none
   past the right edge.
3. No card overflows its column in either mode.
4. Switching back to Standard unwraps.
5. Drag and drop, paging, the filter, counts, the add-card control and
   the column editor all still work.
